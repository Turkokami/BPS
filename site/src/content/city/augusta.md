Augusta is a city of small households — 9,453 of them, averaging under two people each — with a mix of older single-family housing, rental conversions, mobile and manufactured homes, and outbuildings. Rodent work here reflects that mix.

## The job we did here

Rats were digging beneath a trailer and getting inside. We placed exterior bait stations where the travel routes were, filled the holes we found, and the problem was resolved.

That is the shape of nearly every rat job: the animal you see indoors is the symptom, and the burrow system outside is the cause. A trailer, a mobile home or a building on piers is particularly exposed, because the underside is sheltered, undisturbed and usually easy to dig into.

## What the exterior inspection looks for

- Burrows at the foundation, under a slab edge, or beneath a shed, deck, trailer or woodpile
- Smooth runs in the grass, and grease marks where fur repeatedly contacts a surface
- Gaps at the bulkhead, crawlspace vents, and skirting on manufactured homes
- Utility and drain penetrations packed with something soft
- Bins, compost, pet food and bird feeders within travel distance

## What holds when it is sealed

Rats chew. Foam alone fails, loose steel wool fails, and a plastic vent cover fails. What holds is hardware cloth mechanically fastened, sheet metal over chewed edges, mortar at masonry, and copper mesh packed behind sealant in the small gaps. The quote names the material for each repair.

## The neighbourhood conversation

Rats forage across property lines, so an Augusta rat job is often a street problem rather than a property one. Where the burrow system or the food source is next door, the report says so plainly — and we will put it in writing if that helps you raise it with a neighbour or a landlord.

## Everything else

The same city produces ordinary residential work: mice with the first cold, carpenter ants in damp framing, ants and wasps in summer, and tick work on properties with a wooded edge from April through September.
