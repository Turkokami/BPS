Lisbon is a town we want to work in, and we have no Lisbon job on record yet, so this page sticks to what the public figures say about the town's houses and its ticks, and what we would look at first.

## One town, two housing patterns

The village of Lisbon Falls and the rest of Lisbon are one municipality. The Census Bureau draws Lisbon Falls as a census-designated place inside the town, which lets the two be compared, and the comparison is useful.

According to the American Community Survey for 2020–2024, Lisbon as a whole has 3,884 housing units. Of the 908 built in 1939 or earlier, 469 are in Lisbon Falls. So the village, with under half the town's people (4,267 of 9,750 on the same survey), holds just over half of its pre-war houses. It also holds 513 of the town's 960 renter households, and 360 of the 431 units Lisbon has in two-, three- and four-unit buildings.

Outside the village the picture changes. The same survey counts 630 mobile homes in Lisbon, 16.2% of all housing units, and 2,488 detached single-family houses. The town's median home dates from 1973, and the 1960s alone account for 622 units, the most of any decade after the war.

That split matters because the two building types fail in different places.

## What we would check on a Lisbon property

**In a Lisbon Falls house built before 1940:**

- The sill line and the stone or brick foundation, where mortar has gone soft
- Every retrofit hole: oil line, water service, dryer vent, cable entry
- Porch and ell joints where a later addition meets the original frame
- The bulkhead and any crawlspace hatch
- Shared walls and basements, where the building is two or more units

**In a mobile or manufactured home:**

- Skirting gaps, especially where frost has heaved it or the bottom has rotted
- Torn or sagging belly wrap under the floor
- Plumbing, drain and duct penetrations through the floor deck
- The gap around the water heater closet and the furnace flue
- Burrow holes along the skirting line, which point to rats rather than mice

That second list comes from work we have done elsewhere, not in Lisbon. In Augusta we had rats digging under a trailer and getting in from below; the fix was exterior bait stations and filling the burrows. The same pattern is what we would look for first under a Lisbon mobile home. ## Carpenter ants in the older village stock

A house in Lisbon Falls that has stood for ninety years or more has usually had a gutter overflow, a porch roof leak or a window sill that held water at some point. Carpenter ants do not cause that damage; they move into wood that is already soft from it.

Signs worth a call are large black ants indoors in March or April before anything is active outside, small piles of coarse sawdust under a window or along a baseboard, and winged ants coming out of a wall in spring. The [carpenter ant control](/services/carpenter-ant-control/) page describes the treatment. The part that keeps the colony from returning is the moisture finding, and we write that up so it can go to whoever fixes the trim.

## Ticks in Androscoggin County

There is no reliable public tick figure for Lisbon on its own, so the honest starting point is the county.

Maine CDC's 2024 annual tickborne illness report, which it labels preliminary, records 153 Lyme disease cases among Androscoggin County residents. That is a rate of 132.7 per 100,000, still under the statewide 230.0 but up from 40 cases in 2020. The same report counts 45 anaplasmosis cases and 20 babesiosis cases in the county last year.

The UMaine Cooperative Extension Tick Surveillance Program tested 131 blacklegged ticks submitted from Androscoggin County in 2025. Fifty of them (38.2%) carried *Borrelia burgdorferi*, the Lyme bacterium, and 20 (15.3%) carried *Babesia microti*. The lab notes that these are ticks the public chose to send in, so they show what the ticks here carry rather than how many there are.

For a Lisbon lot with a wooded or field edge, the work is concentrated where the mowed grass meets leaf litter and brush. The [tick control](/services/tick-control/) page sets out what a season of visits involves, and the [Maine pest calendar](/library/maine-pest-calendar/) shows when ticks, mosquitoes and wasps each peak through the year.

## Before the cold sets in

Mice look for warm buildings as Maine nights turn cold in the fall. Under a mobile home, warm air leaks out through the floor deck, and that is where they will try first. In a village house it is the sill. An inspection before the first hard frost finds those openings while they can still be closed from the outside.
