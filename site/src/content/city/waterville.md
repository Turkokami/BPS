Waterville is the northern edge of the routine service area and a city that produces a particular kind of work: multi-unit rental buildings, older housing, and problems that cross between households.

## Why the building matters more than the unit

Fewer than half of Waterville's occupied homes are owner-occupied, and the rental stock includes plenty of buildings where units share walls, plumbing runs and corridors. Bed bugs and German cockroaches travel those routes. They also travel in laundry baskets and on furniture, which is why an infestation that appears in one apartment is a building question from the first day rather than the third month.

## The job we did here

A property-management company called us about bed bugs in one unit. The inspection found a large infestation in that unit — and evidence that it had already spread into connecting units. We treated the affected units together rather than one at a time, and the infestation was eliminated.

That sequencing is the whole difference. Treating the reporting unit alone would have produced a quiet fortnight and then a second call from next door.

## A city of two- and four-unit houses

Waterville's multi-unit housing is mostly small buildings rather than big blocks. The U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2020–2024 five-year estimates) counts 7,312 housing units in the city, and only 2,990 — 40.9% — are detached houses, the lowest share of the four river cities this site covers. Two-unit buildings account for 1,252 units (17.1%) and three- and four-unit buildings for another 950 (13.0%), so close to a third of the city lives in a house shared with between one and three other households.

That shape changes the scope in a useful way. In a duplex or a small multi-family house, "connecting units" is a short list: the unit above or below, the one through the party wall, and the shared spaces. The cellar, the common stairwell, the shared laundry and the attic belong to everyone and are often checked by no one, which makes them the first places the inspection goes for mouse nesting and for signs of bed bugs crossing between households.

The age profile is unusual too. The same survey dates 2,239 units (30.6%) to 1939 or earlier, but the next-largest group is the 1970s, with 1,579 units, or 21.6% — a bigger share for that one decade than in Lewiston, Auburn or Augusta. A 1970s apartment building and a pre-war two-family house need different inspections, and the scope says which one it was written for.

In the older two-family stock, the inspection spends its time on the shared cellar, the stone or brick foundation, the back stairs and any porch built onto the original frame, because those are where mice get in and where damp timber invites carpenter ants. In a 1970s building the questions move indoors: the plumbing chases and electrical runs that link kitchens and bathrooms stacked above each other, the gaps behind baseboard heating, and the common corridor. Those internal routes are what German cockroaches and bed bugs use, and they are why one unit's problem becomes three.

## What a building-level scope contains

1. The reporting unit and every unit sharing a wall, ceiling or floor with it, inspected
2. Treatment dates for every confirmed unit, in one window
3. Second-round dates two to three weeks later, because eggs survive the first round
4. A written record of any unit that could not be accessed, and when it will be re-booked
5. A clear split of what the manager arranges and what the tenant does

## Ticks around Waterville

Waterville's numbers from UMaine Cooperative Extension's Tick Lab stand out among the four cities on this site. Of 36 ticks submitted from the town, 16 (44.4%) tested positive for *Borrelia burgdorferi*, the Lyme bacterium, and 10 (27.8%) for *Anaplasma*, which causes anaplasmosis — the highest proportions for either organism among Waterville, Augusta, Lewiston and Auburn in the lab's town data. The sample is small and self-selected, so read it as a sign of what local ticks carry rather than a measured rate.

Across Kennebec County, Maine CDC's 2024 reportable-disease summary lists a Lyme disease rate of 229.6 cases per 100,000 residents, effectively level with the state's 230.1. Properties with a wooded or overgrown boundary are where yard treatment earns its keep; the <a href="/services/tick-control/">tick control</a> page explains the April-to-September programme.
