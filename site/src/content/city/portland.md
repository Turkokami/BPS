Portland is the largest city in Maine and it is the one place in this service area where the building matters more than the house. According to the Census Bureau, fewer than half of Portland's occupied homes are owner-occupied, and that changes how pest work has to be organised here.

## Why the rental share decides the job

In an owner-occupied house, a bed bug infestation is a household problem with a household solution. In a three-unit building it is a building problem: the insects travel through wall voids, along pipe chases, under doors and along corridors, and they are carried between units in laundry baskets and on furniture.

We scope a Portland bed bug job to include the units sharing a wall, a floor or a ceiling with the one that reported it, and we treat the confirmed ones in the same window rather than one at a time as complaints arrive. Buildings that do it the other way round spend more over a year and lose their tenants' patience long before the insects are gone.

## What we get called for in Portland

- Bed bugs in apartments and in owner-occupied condos
- German cockroaches in kitchens, often in buildings where several units share plumbing runs
- Mice in older housing stock, entering low at the sill and travelling up inside the walls
- Ants — pavement ants at slab edges and carpenter ants where a gutter has been overflowing onto trim
- Ticks in the yards on the city's edges, especially where lawns meet the wooded margins

## The older housing stock

A median Portland home is worth close to half a million dollars, which tells you something about the age and desirability of the building stock rather than its condition. Old houses in this city have been subdivided, re-plumbed, re-wired and re-roofed, and every one of those changes left a penetration somewhere that nobody sealed. That is where mice come in.

Rodent work in Portland is therefore mostly exterior: sill gaps, bulkhead seals, utility penetrations, and the gap under a garage door that has worn back a quarter of an inch. We list every opening we find, quote the sealing separately from the trapping, and say plainly when something belongs to a carpenter rather than to us.

## Working in a building you do not own

If you are a tenant, you can call us and we will tell you what we see, but the treatment has to be arranged by whoever holds the building. If you are a landlord or a property manager, we will put the scope in writing: the units to inspect, the treatment dates, the second-round dates, and a record of any unit we could not access. That last item is the one that explains most buildings that are still seeing activity six months later.
