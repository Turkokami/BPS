Oxford covers nearly 39 square miles and holds about 4,200 people, which means most properties here have trees on at least one boundary and a good many have water on another. Thompson Lake, which the Department of Inland Fisheries and Wildlife puts at more than 4,400 acres, is shared with Otisfield and Poland, and the Census Bureau counts one Oxford home in five as held for seasonal or occasional use.

## What Oxford's housing looks like on paper

The Census Bureau's American Community Survey for 2020–2024 describes a town that is very different from the cities we serve:

| Measure | Oxford | What it means for pests |
|---|---|---|
| Housing units held for seasonal, recreational or occasional use | 399 of 1,997 (20.0%) | One building in five can sit unwatched for months |
| Mobile homes | 467 (23.4%) | Skirting and under-floor service runs become the main rodent route |
| Owner-occupied homes | 1,386 of 1,562 occupied (88.7%) | The person who calls usually decides the work |
| Units built in 1939 or earlier | 218 (10.9%) | Old masonry is the exception here rather than the rule |

That last row is worth noticing: the busiest decade for building in Oxford was the 1970s, which accounts for 539 units, more than a quarter of the stock. The problems here are less about century-old sills and more about where a house or camp sits and how long it is left alone.

## Camps and closed-up buildings

A building that nobody enters between October and May is the single most reliable rodent habitat in this county. It is warm enough, it is undisturbed, and it usually contains exactly what a mouse wants: soft furnishings, stored bedding, paper, and no human traffic at all.

With roughly one Oxford housing unit in five counted by the Census Bureau as seasonal or occasional, this is not a niche job here. The work that prevents it happens before the building closes rather than after it opens:

1. Seal the exterior while the weather still allows a proper repair — sills, vents, utility penetrations, the gap under the door.
2. Remove or seal anything that can be nested in, and store fabrics in hard containers rather than bags.
3. Deal with the woodpile: off the ground, away from the wall, and never stacked against the building over winter.
4. Set monitoring so that what happens over the winter is visible in spring rather than guessed at.
5. Book the opening-up inspection for when you return, so the first weekend is not spent cleaning.

If a camp has already wintered with mice in it, the [attic clean-out and sanitation](/services/attic-cleanout-and-sanitation/) work and the [rodent exclusion](/services/rodent-exclusion/) work are usually done together, so that the clean space is also a sealed one.

## Mobile homes

Nearly a quarter of Oxford's housing is mobile homes, which is a building type with its own pest logic. The living space sits above a void enclosed by skirting, and that void is crossed by water, sewer and electrical lines that each need a hole in the floor. Where the skirting has lifted, split or been pushed out by frost, mice have a sheltered crawlspace under the whole house and a choice of ways up into it. We inspect the skirting line and the underside penetrations first, and we write down which repairs are skirting work rather than pest work.

## Living against the tree line

Properties here mostly end at woods, brush or water, and that boundary is where the pest pressure sits. Ticks concentrate in the leaf litter and the first few feet of cover. Rodents travel along stone walls and wood edges. Wasps and hornets build in eaves, sheds and under decks that back onto shade.

The forest edge matters for carpenter ants too. UMaine Cooperative Extension notes that the closer a forest with rotten logs is to a building, the higher the risk of a carpenter ant infestation, and that sill areas are especially inviting where soil touches the wood — which describes a good many lake and woodlot properties in this town.

Tick numbers in this county have moved quickly. Maine CDC's 2024 report, on preliminary figures, counts 131 Lyme disease cases among Oxford County residents, a rate of 218.2 per 100,000, compared with 43 cases and a rate of 74.2 in 2020. The UMaine Cooperative Extension Tick Lab found the Lyme bacterium in 44 of the 109 blacklegged ticks from Oxford County that it tested in 2025, or 40.4%. For a house or camp backing onto woods, [tick control](/services/tick-control/) at the lawn edge is worth planning rather than reacting to.

That is a habitat conversation as much as a treatment one, and the report you get from us will say which of the two would do more for your property.
