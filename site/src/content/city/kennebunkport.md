Kennebunkport is a town of detached houses, many of them empty for part of the year. That pattern, more than anything about the coast, decides what pest work here looks like. A house that stands closed from fall to late spring offers mice and overwintering insects months without a person walking through it, and the problem is usually discovered by the owner on the first weekend back.

Two of those rows set this town apart from its neighbor. Kennebunk has more than twice the housing, yet the Census Bureau estimates a similar number of seasonal homes in each — 928 there, 885 here. And almost nine in ten Kennebunkport units are detached houses, with very few apartments; the survey counts only 343 renter households in the whole town. The seasonal estimates carry wide margins of error, but even at their edges the seasonal share here stays well above Kennebunk's.

## Why an empty house is the job

A house closed for the winter has nearly everything a mouse wants: shelter, quiet, upholstered furniture and mattresses for nesting, and nobody to notice. By the time the owners return, the evidence is droppings along the baseboards, chewed packaging in a pantry, and sometimes soiled insulation in the attic or a crawlspace.

The cheaper point to act is before closing. A closing-up visit works around the outside of the building:

- the sill line and foundation, including any crawlspace vents and bulkhead
- utility entries for water, power, cable and heat pump lines
- the garage door seals and the door from the garage into the house
- the roofline, soffit returns and gable vents
- exterior bait stations, secured, along the routes rodents travel

An opening-up visit in spring then checks the stations and the interior for anything that got through. Where it has, [rodent exclusion](/services/rodent-exclusion/) closes the remaining gaps and [attic clean-outs](/services/attic-cleanout-and-sanitation/) deal with insulation that cannot be saved.

## The age of the houses

Kennebunkport's housing is not as old as its reputation might suggest. The Census Bureau's survey puts 380 units, or 13.1%, in buildings from 1939 or earlier, a smaller share than Kennebunk's 18.8%. The median Kennebunkport house dates from 1982, and no single decade since 1950 accounts for more than about 15% of the stock.

What that means in practice is a mix. The older houses have the retrofit gaps that come with a century of rewiring and re-plumbing. On the newer ones, worn garage door seals and the joint where a deck meets the house are the first places we would look.

## Summer pests in a part-time house

Wasps, hornets and yellowjackets build through the summer and peak late. In a house that is lived in only on weekends, a nest in the eaves or behind a shutter can get large before anyone notices it. Our [wasps and hornets in Maine](/library/wasps-and-hornets-in-maine/) guide explains which nests are worth treating and which can be left to die out in the fall. When a nest does need to go, [wasp and hornet control](/services/wasp-and-hornet-control/) treats it at the source.

The other late-season visitors are the insects that move into wall voids to overwinter — cluster flies and their relatives. In a house that closes in fall, they emerge on the first warm days indoors and are found in windows the following spring. The [overwintering insects](/library/overwintering-insects-in-maine-houses/) page covers the late-summer sealing that keeps them out.

## Ticks around the yard

The Town of Kennebunkport describes Goose Rocks Beach as a three-mile stretch of beach, and on open sand tick exposure is low. Yards are a different story where lawn runs into brush and leaf litter.

York County's tickborne illness figures go beyond Lyme. Maine CDC's 2024 report, on preliminary data, counts 79 anaplasmosis cases, 17 babesiosis cases and one Powassan virus case among county residents, alongside 286 Lyme cases. The UMaine Cooperative Extension Tick Lab found the anaplasmosis agent in 7.9% and the babesiosis agent in 12.3% of the county's blacklegged ticks that it tested in 2025. [Tick control](/services/tick-control/) treats the yard edge on a schedule from spring into fall.
