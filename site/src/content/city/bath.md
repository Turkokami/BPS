Bath is a small city by population and a dense one by Maine standards: roughly 8,800 people on nine square miles of land, with houses close together and most of them owner-occupied. That density is the thing that shapes pest work here.

## Close neighbours, shared pressure

When properties sit on small lots with shared fence lines, mature trees and adjoining garages, a rodent or carpenter ant population is rarely confined to one address. A woodpile against a wall, an unsecured bin, a neglected shed two doors down — each of them sustains pressure that arrives at your foundation regardless of what you do inside your own house.

We will say so when we see it. It is an awkward conversation and it is more useful than another month of trapping.

## The new-owner inspection

The most common way a Bath household discovers a pest problem is by buying the house. A home inspection covers structure and systems; it is not a pest inspection, and a seller who lived with a mouse problem for three winters is not obliged to describe it.

We did exactly this job here about a year ago: a family had just bought a home, found it was dealing with both ants and mice, and treated it themselves with retail aerosols before calling. That is the normal sequence and it is the wrong one, because the products scatter the population and make the evidence harder to read.

If you have just moved in, the inspection to ask for is the one that covers:

- Sill line, bulkhead and crawlspace access on the exterior
- Utility penetrations — water service, electrical entry, dryer vent, hose bibs
- Attic insulation for travel paths and droppings
- Window and door framing for carpenter ant frass
- Basement joists and any wood in ground contact

## What the seasons bring here

Coastal Sagadahoc County runs a compressed pest year. Carpenter ant activity indoors in late winter usually means a colony inside the structure rather than visitors. Ant swarms and the first ticks arrive with the spring thaw. Midsummer is wasps and mosquitoes. Then the first cold nights move rodents toward buildings, and a house that was quiet all summer starts producing noise overhead in October.

Sealing work done in September is prevention. The same work in January is a repair with the animals already inside.
