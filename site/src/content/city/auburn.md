Auburn sits across the Androscoggin from Lewiston and behaves differently. It is more owner-occupied, more suburban in layout, and heavier on single-family homes with attached garages and outbuildings — and the pest work reflects that.

## The garage is usually the way in

We did a whole-home inspection here where several entry points turned up, and the significant one was the garage door seals at the point where the garage joins the house. The seals were replaced, exterior bait stations went in where the travel routes were, and the problem stopped.

That is not an unusual finding in Auburn, it is the normal one. The bottom seal on an overhead door wears at the corners first, and a quarter-inch gap at the corner of a garage door is an open invitation in October. Once an animal is in the garage it is already inside the building envelope, and the interior door into the house is rarely sealed as well as the front door is.

## What a whole-home inspection covers here

- Garage door bottom and side seals, and the door from the garage into the house
- Sill line and foundation, including the bulkhead
- Utility penetrations: water service, electrical entry, dryer vent, hose bibs
- Roofline: soffit returns, drip edge, gable and ridge vent screening
- Outbuildings, and anything stored against an exterior wall
- Grounds: woodpiles, feeders, compost, and sheds sitting on soil

## Auburn's houses are older than they look

From the street, a lot of Auburn reads as mid-century suburb. The numbers say otherwise. The U.S. Census Bureau's American Community Survey (2020–2024 five-year estimates) counts 11,136 housing units in the city, and 4,244 of them — 38.1% — were built in 1939 or earlier. That is the highest pre-war share of the four river cities on this site; Lewiston, Augusta and Waterville all sit between roughly 29% and 32% on the same survey. Only 148 Auburn units were built in 2020 or later.

A house of that age has been worked on many times. New wiring, a replacement boiler, a heat pump line set, a cable run, a dryer vent moved when the laundry came up from the cellar — each one went through the shell, and each one left a gap that was either packed with something soft or not packed at all. When we inspect a pre-war Auburn house, those retrofit holes are as likely to be the entry as the garage, and the report lists every one with what it should be closed with.

The same Census survey puts 5,843 Auburn units, or 52.5%, in detached single-family houses — again the largest share of the four. That is why exclusion here is mostly a perimeter job. There is no neighbour's unit on the other side of the wall to worry about; there is a foundation, a sill, a roofline and a garage, and once they are closed the house is closed. ## Carpenter ants in the older stock

Auburn's older neighbourhoods produce the same carpenter ant work as Lewiston — colonies in sills and window framing where water has been getting in. On a house approaching its centenary, the places we check first are predictable: the sill plate where soil or mulch has crept up to meet the siding, the joint where a later porch or mudroom was added onto the original frame, and window frames that have been repainted for decades rather than replaced.

Large black ants seen indoors in late winter, piles of fine wood shavings under a window, or a rustling in a wall at night are all worth a call. What finishes the job is not the treatment alone but the moisture note: if the leak that softened the timber is still there, the next colony will find it. ## Ticks: what the local evidence says

Tick pressure is higher here than in the denser parts of the corridor, because more properties back onto woods or field edges. There is local evidence for what those ticks carry. UMaine Cooperative Extension's Tick Lab has tested 83 ticks submitted from Auburn across its published seasons, and 33 of them — 39.8% — were positive for *Borrelia burgdorferi*, the bacterium that causes Lyme disease. Ten carried *Anaplasma* and eight carried *Babesia*.

Two cautions go with those numbers, and the lab states the first one itself: a positive tick does not mean the person it bit was infected, and a negative one does not mean they were not. The second is that these are ticks people chose to send in, which is not a random sample of the town.

The human figures are lower than the coast but far from negligible. Maine CDC's summary of reportable infectious diseases for 2024 recorded 153 Lyme disease cases among Androscoggin County residents, a rate of 132.7 per 100,000 people, against a statewide rate of 230.1.

For a yard backing onto woods, the useful work is at the edge: the strip where lawn meets leaf litter, stone walls, and the shaded ground under shrubs. Tick and mosquito work runs monthly from April to September, which is the window when it does anything useful.
