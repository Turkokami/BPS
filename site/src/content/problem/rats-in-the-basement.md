A rat in a Maine basement is a structural access problem, not a housekeeping one. Rats chew openings rather than waiting to find them, and they follow the paths that already run into the building.

## What we look for outside

- Burrows at the foundation, under a slab edge, or beneath a shed, deck or woodpile
- Smooth runs in the grass or along the foundation line
- Grease marks where fur repeatedly contacts a surface
- Gaps at the bulkhead, the crawlspace vents, or the garage slab edge
- Utility and drain penetrations packed with something soft
- Unsecured bins, compost, pet food and bird feeders within travel distance

## What we look for inside

- Droppings along the walls rather than in the open
- Gnawing on wood edges, plastic pipe, or the corners of stored boxes
- Nesting material pulled from insulation or stored fabric
- Openings around the sump, the sewer clean-out, or the water service

## The order of work

1. Identify and document entries, inside and out.
2. Remove the animals present with traps placed on the confirmed runs.
3. Seal with material rats cannot chew: hardware cloth, metal, mortar where appropriate. Foam alone fails.
4. Remove the food source that is sustaining the pressure, or say plainly whose it is.
5. Re-inspect, because rats test a repair.

## Why the outside work decides the result

A basement is the room where you noticed the problem. The burrow system, the food source and the travel route are outside it. Work that stops at the foundation wall treats the symptom and leaves the population intact, which is why a trapping-only response in a rat job reads as success for about a month.

The exterior sequence — document burrows, remove or secure the food source, seal with material that cannot be chewed, then re-inspect — is what changes the pressure. Where the food source belongs to a neighbour or a commercial bin nearby, the honest version of the report says so, because nothing on your side of the line solves it alone.

## Telling rats from mice, quickly

| Sign | Mouse | Rat |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Droppings | Rice-grain size, scattered widely | Two to three times larger, often clustered |
| Gnawing | Small, neat, fine tooth marks | Coarse, wider, cuts through soft plastic and lead |
| Grease marks | Faint if any | Obvious smears along wall runs |
| Entry size | Gap the width of a pencil | Chews an opening if there is not one |
| Where they nest | Inside voids, insulation, cabinets | Burrows outside, crawlspaces, under sheds and decks |

The identification matters because it decides where the work happens. Mouse work runs inward, rat work runs outward.

## What draws rats to a property

- Bin lids that do not close, or bags left out overnight
- Bird feeders, particularly the spill underneath them
- Pet food or livestock feed stored in soft containers
- Compost with cooked food in it
- Fallen fruit under a tree
- Dense ground cover, woodpiles and sheds sitting directly on soil
- A neighbouring commercial bin, a restaurant, or ongoing construction

Most of these are cheap to fix and none of them is treatable. That is why the report names them.

## The awkward part

Rats forage across property lines, and a great many Maine rat jobs are really neighbourhood jobs. If the burrow system is under a shed two doors down and the food is an unsecured bin next door, treating your basement alone will hold the line and nothing more. We will say that plainly, in writing if it helps you have the conversation, because a polite note from a neighbour is often more effective than another month of trapping.

## Timeline

Removal on a typical residential rat job runs over two to three weeks of trapping and re-checks, with sealing done as the entries are confirmed. Rats test repairs, so a re-inspection is part of the work rather than an optional extra.

## Sealing materials that hold

Rats chew. Expanding foam on its own, steel wool left loose, plastic vent covers and silicone across a wide gap are all temporary. What holds is hardware cloth mechanically fastened, sheet metal over chewed edges, mortar or concrete patch at masonry, and copper mesh packed tight behind sealant in small gaps. Every repair we quote names the material, so you can check it was done that way.
