Noise overhead at night is the most common way a Maine household finds out it has rodents, and it is usually reported weeks after the animals moved in.

| What you hear | When | Most often |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Light scratching, scurrying, faint squeaks | After dark, especially the first hours | Mice |
| Gliding thumps, quick runs, chirps | Dusk and night | Flying squirrels |
| Heavy rolling, gnawing, daytime movement | Daylight, early morning | Grey squirrels |
| Slow heavy movement, vocalisations | Night | Raccoons |

## What to do first

1. Note the time of day the noise happens, and write it down for two or three nights.
2. Look for droppings along the attic edges, in insulation valleys, and on top of the ceiling joists.
3. Look outside at the roofline for gaps: open soffit returns, lifted drip edge, unscreened gable vents, a gap where the chimney meets the roof.
4. Do not seal an entry point until you know the animals are out — sealing an animal inside a wall is a worse problem than the one you started with.

## Why the species changes the job

Mice generally enter low — sill gaps, utility penetrations, bulkheads — and travel up inside walls to the attic, so mouse work is interior voids plus low exterior sealing. Squirrels enter at the roofline and need exclusion work at height, sometimes with a one-way door. Bats are protected and are a different conversation entirely, with a legally constrained exclusion window.

That is why the inspection identifies the species from droppings, gnawing and travel marks before anything is quoted.

## The cost of waiting

An attic that has been occupied through a Maine winter typically shows compressed and soiled insulation in the travel paths, and sometimes gnawed wiring. The remediation is sequenced: exclude, remove, then remediate. New insulation installed while the roofline is still open is new insulation with a runway through it.

## What the inspection tells you that the noise cannot

Sound travels along framing, so the corner of the ceiling where you hear scratching is often two bays away from where the animal is. The evidence does not move: droppings collect where animals travel and rest, insulation is compressed along the runs, and grease marks build at the pinch points they squeeze through. Reading that evidence is what produces an entry list rather than a guess.

In most Maine attics the entry list is short and repetitive — an open soffit return at a roof junction, a gable vent screen that has rusted through, a gap behind a gutter where the drip edge lifted, a chimney flashing gap. Those are the same four items on house after house, which is why an inspection that ends without naming any of them has not finished.

## The four openings that account for most attic entries

On Maine houses the same handful of details turn up again and again:

1. **Open soffit returns.** Where a roof plane meets a wall, the boxed corner is often left open inside. From the ground it looks finished; from a ladder it is a doorway.
2. **Lifted drip edge behind gutters.** Ice damming lifts the metal, and the gap that opens runs the length of the eave.
3. **Gable and ridge vent screening.** Screen rusts. A hole the size of a coin is enough.
4. **Chimney and flashing junctions.** Mortar fails, flashing lifts, and the gap sits in the warmest part of the roofline.

A fifth, less obvious route: mice entering at ground level and travelling up inside a wall cavity to the attic without ever crossing a living space. That is why an attic problem is often solved at the sill.

## Cleaning up afterwards

Do not dry-sweep or vacuum rodent droppings in an enclosed space. Ventilate first, dampen the material, and remove it wet, wearing gloves and a mask. Deer mice are the species associated with hantavirus in the United States, and while cases are rare, the precaution costs nothing.

Insulation in the travel paths is usually compressed and soiled after a season of occupation. Not every attic needs stripping and we will say when yours does not. When it does, the sequence is exclude, remove, then remediate — new insulation over an open roofline is money spent on a future mouse runway.
