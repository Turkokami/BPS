Rodent damage in an attic is cumulative and invisible until somebody goes up there. The insulation loses its loft where animals have been travelling, droppings work down through the bays, and any exposed wiring is a question worth answering before anything else.

## What the assessment covers

- Which bays are contaminated and which are intact
- Depth and condition of the remaining insulation, and whether it is still doing its job
- Droppings and nesting material through the space and in stored items
- Gnawing on wiring, ducting, and any plastic or soft pipework
- Whether the roofline and the rest of the structure have actually been sealed
- Ventilation — soffit and ridge airflow, which is often blocked by displaced insulation

## The order of work, and why it is not negotiable

1. **Exclude.** Every opening found is closed, with materials that hold. Until this is done, nothing else is worth paying for.
2. **Remove.** The animals inside come out first, and activity is confirmed to have stopped.
3. **Clean out.** Contaminated insulation and nesting material are removed damp, bagged in the space, and taken away rather than carried loose through the house.
4. **Sanitise.** The affected surfaces are treated, and the space ventilated.
5. **Re-insulate.** New material goes in last, to the depth the space actually needs, with baffles kept clear.

## Safety, briefly and plainly

Deer mice are the species associated with hantavirus in the United States, and the risk in a roof space comes from disturbed dust rather than from contact. That is why the material is wetted before removal, why a mask and gloves are not optional, and why we do not recommend a homeowner dry-sweeping an attic they have just found droppings in.

## What you get afterwards

A record of what was removed, what was treated, what was installed and to what depth, plus photographs of the space before and after. If ventilation or wiring needs a different trade, that is named rather than left for you to discover.

## When we will tell you not to bother

A light scattering of droppings in a corner of an otherwise sound attic does not justify a clean-out. Neither does a single season of low-level activity in a space with good insulation. In both cases the money is better spent on the sealing that stops it happening again, and we would rather say that than sell the bigger job.
