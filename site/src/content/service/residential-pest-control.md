Most Maine households do not have one pest problem; they have a sequence of them that arrives on a schedule. A programme exists so somebody is watching that sequence instead of you reacting to it.

## What the year looks like

| Season | What tends to arrive | What the visit covers |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Late winter | Interior carpenter ant activity, overwintering rodents | Interior inspection, colony work, entry-point check |
| Spring | Ant swarms, first tick activity, cluster flies | Perimeter, ant treatment, tick edge treatment begins |
| Summer | Mosquitoes, ticks, wasps, stored-product pests | Habitat work, staged exterior treatment |
| Autumn | Rodents moving indoors, spiders, boxelder and stink bugs | Exclusion review, sealing, rodent programme |

## How a programme is built

The first visit is a full inspection, inside and out, and it produces two things: a treatment plan and an exclusion list. The exclusion list is the part that shrinks the plan over time — gaps sealed in year one are gaps nobody treats in year three.

Visits are scheduled to the season rather than evenly across the calendar, because a rodent exclusion check in July is worth less than the same visit in September.

## What you get in writing

Every visit produces a short report: what was inspected, what was found, what was applied and where, and what is recommended next. When something is outside pest control — a failed sill, a roof leak, a crawlspace moisture problem — it is named in the report rather than quietly treated around.

## What a first year usually looks like

The first year of a programme is front-loaded: a full inspection, the exclusion work that comes out of it, and treatment for whatever is already active. By the second year the exclusion list is shorter, the visits are lighter, and the conversation moves from fixing to watching. That is the shape a programme should have. If your costs are identical in year three to year one, nothing structural was ever fixed.

## Questions worth asking any company, including us

1. Which Maine applicator categories do you hold, and what is your licence number?
2. Who will actually be at my house, and will it be the same person each visit?
3. What are the cancellation terms, in writing?
4. What is included in a visit, and what triggers an extra charge?
5. What will you tell me about my house that is not pest control?

A company that answers all five in plain language is a company you can work with. The Maine Board of Pesticides Control publishes a public directory of licensed commercial applicators, and you are entitled to check any answer you get.

## What a first visit includes

The first visit is longer than the ones that follow, because it is the one that produces the plan.

- A full interior walk: basement or crawlspace, mechanical room, kitchen, bathrooms, attic access, and any space where activity has been noticed.
- A full exterior walk: foundation, sill line, doors and windows, roofline, vents and penetrations, decks and porches, outbuildings, woodpiles, grounds.
- Identification of anything active, from evidence rather than description.
- Treatment of what is present, in the licensed category the work falls under.
- A written report: what was inspected, what was found, what was applied and where, what is recommended, and what belongs to another trade.

## What the visits after that look like

Shorter, and more targeted. Each visit checks the conditions that produced the last problem, re-treats where seasonally appropriate, and updates the exclusion list. Interior work happens when something needs it rather than as a routine — spraying the inside of a house on a schedule is rarely the right answer, and a programme that does it by default is selling visits rather than results.

## Exclusion, and why it shrinks your bill

The exclusion list is the part of a residential programme that should get shorter every year. Each sealed opening is a route that does not need treating again. A programme that costs the same in year three as in year one has not fixed anything structural, and that is a fair question to ask any company, including us.

## Pets, children and product choice

Tell us what lives in the house. Product selection, placement and timing all change around children, pets, chickens, fish tanks and vegetable gardens. Anything placed indoors goes where it cannot be reached; anything applied outdoors is placed with your garden and your animals in mind, and the areas we are leaving alone are written into the plan before we start.

## What we will tell you that is not pest control

Most recurring pest problems in Maine houses are moisture problems or structural problems wearing a pest costume. A wet sill, a failed gutter line, a crawlspace with no vapour barrier, a garage door seal worn back half an inch. Those go in the report with photographs, even though fixing them is not work we sell.

## Scheduling and access

Exterior work does not need you home; interior work does. Visits are scheduled with you rather than assigned, and we will text before arriving. If access fails, the visit is re-booked rather than marked complete.

## What happens if something comes back

Call. Between-visit problems on an active programme are part of the programme, not a new job, and the point of the written record is that the next visit starts from what was already found rather than from scratch.

## What is not in a programme

Wildlife — raccoons, squirrels, bats, birds — is separate work with separate rules, and bats in particular are protected with a legally constrained exclusion window. Carpentry, roofing, gutter and moisture repairs are named in the report and quoted by the relevant trade. Lawn and ornamental treatments sit in different licence categories and are not part of a structural pest programme unless we hold and state those categories.

Saying so up front avoids the most common disappointment in this trade: a customer who believed a general programme covered everything that walks, flies or leaks.

## Leaving

If you want to stop, say so and it stops. Ask any pest control company for the cancellation terms in writing before you sign, including ours. A programme that is hard to leave is a programme that stopped competing for your business.
