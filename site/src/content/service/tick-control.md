Tick pressure in Maine is not evenly spread across a property. It is concentrated at the boundary — the two or three metres where mown lawn gives way to leaf litter, brush, stone wall or woods. That is where the humidity holds, where the mice and chipmunks that carry the nymphs travel, and where a dog or a child brushes past on the way to somewhere else.

## Where the ticks actually are

- The wood line, and the first few feet of leaf litter inside it
- Stone walls and the vegetation growing out of them, which hold both moisture and rodents
- Ground cover and ornamental beds along the foundation
- Tall grass at the edges of a field, a paddock or a septic mound
- Woodpiles, brush piles and the shaded ground beneath them
- Anywhere a bird feeder concentrates rodent traffic

The open middle of a sunny, mown lawn is the least hospitable part of a Maine yard for a tick. That is useful, because it means the goal is not to treat everything — it is to treat the edge and widen the part of the yard that already works against them.

## What a treatment does

A perimeter tick treatment applies a labelled product to the edge habitat and the transition zone. It reduces what is present in the treated band for a period of weeks, which matters most during the nymph window when the risk of an unnoticed bite is highest. It does not sterilise a property, and it does not stop ticks arriving on deer that walk the tree line at night.

That is why the work is staged: treat before the nymph peak, again through the season, and again for the autumn adult surge, with the timing set by the weather rather than the calendar.

## The habitat half of the job

The part of tick control nobody sells hard enough is the part that costs nothing. A three-foot dry barrier of wood chips or gravel where the lawn meets the woods is a meaningful obstacle to tick movement. Moving the swing set and the seating area out of the shade at the wood line changes where people spend time. Clearing leaf litter in spring removes overwintering habitat. Keeping the feeder away from the house moves rodent traffic away from the house.

We will write those items into the report with the treatment plan, and we will say when the habitat work would do more for you than a fourth application.

## Pets, livestock and the same yard

A dog moving through the edge habitat several times a day is the most efficient tick transport a property has. Yard treatment reduces what the dog encounters; it does not replace a veterinary preventative, and we will say so. Where there are chickens, horses or a vegetable garden, the treatment plan changes — products are chosen and placed for those conditions, and areas that should not be treated are marked and left alone.

## How a season is staged

A tick programme in Maine is not one date on a calendar. The ground here is workable at different times in Lewiston and in the hills above Rumford, and a warm March pulls adult ticks out weeks before a cold one does. The staging that works:

1. **Early season.** Walk the property, mark the edge habitat, and get the first application down once the ground has thawed and the adults from last autumn are moving again.
2. **Nymph window.** Late spring into early summer is when the nymphs — poppy-seed sized, easy to miss, and the stage most associated with disease transmission — are active. This is the round that matters most for households with children and dogs.
3. **Midsummer.** Pressure drops in hot dry spells and rebounds after rain. Treatment is adjusted to conditions rather than applied on a fixed date.
4. **Autumn.** Adults surge again as temperatures fall, and they stay active into late autumn whenever the ground is unfrozen. The last round of the year covers the period when people are raking, stacking wood and clearing beds.

## When the programme runs

Tick and mosquito service runs monthly from April through September, which is the window in which it does anything useful. Outside that window the honest advice is habitat work and a spring booking rather than a winter application.

## Ticks and dogs

A dog is the most efficient tick transport on a property, and it moves through exactly the habitat where the ticks are. Yard treatment reduces what the dog encounters, and a veterinary preventative handles what it still picks up. Both together work; either alone leaves a gap. We will say that even though only one half of it is ours to sell.

## Deer, rodents and the limit of a property line

Ticks arrive on hosts. Deer move along the tree line at night, and the white-footed mice and chipmunks that carry the nymphs live in the stone walls and litter at the edge. Nothing applied inside your boundary changes what walks across it. What treatment and habitat work do together is reduce how much of that traffic converts into ticks questing in the parts of the yard your family uses.

## What ticks are doing through a Maine year

Ticks are not a summer pest here. Adult deer ticks are active whenever the ground is unfrozen, which in a mild winter means January thaw days as well as November. That is why the advice on this page is seasonal rather than monthly, and why an autumn round matters as much as a spring one.

The rough shape of the year on a Maine property:

- **Thaw to late spring.** Adults that overwintered become active and quest at knee height along the edge. People pick these up raking, clearing brush and walking dogs.
- **Late spring into summer.** Nymphs emerge. They are poppy-seed sized, they quest low, and they are the stage most often missed on a body check. This is the window that shapes the treatment plan.
- **High summer.** Larvae appear; pressure in dry heat drops and rebounds after rain.
- **Autumn.** A second adult surge, coinciding with yard clean-up, wood stacking and hunting season.

## Checking a person and a dog

Habitat and treatment reduce exposure. A body check ends it. After time in the edge habitat, check the hairline and scalp, behind the ears, the armpits, the waistband, behind the knees, and between the toes. On dogs, check the ears, the collar line, between the pads, and the belly.

Remove an attached tick with fine-tipped tweezers: grasp close to the skin, pull steadily upward without twisting, and clean the site. Do not burn it, smother it or paint it with anything. If you develop a rash or a fever in the weeks afterwards, that is a conversation for your doctor, and it is worth being able to say roughly when the bite happened — which is why checking regularly beats checking occasionally.

## What we will not claim

We will not tell you a treated yard is a safe yard. Ticks arrive on animals that do not respect property lines, and no product applied inside your boundary changes deer movement. What we will tell you is how much of the pressure is coming from your own edge habitat, what changing it would do, and what a staged treatment adds on top. Anyone promising a tick-free property in Maine is selling a claim they cannot support.

## Licensing, and why the category matters

Tick and biting-fly work in Maine sits in commercial applicator category 7E, which is separate from the 7A category that covers structural pest control. The Maine Board of Pesticides Control is explicit that commercial applicators may only apply in the categories they are licensed for, and it publishes a directory of licensed commercial applicators that anyone can check before hiring.
