The cockroach that causes real problems in Maine is the German cockroach. It is small, tan, fast, and it lives its whole life inside the building — in the warm tight spaces near food and water. Occasional larger roaches wandering in from outside are a different and much simpler problem.

## Where they actually live

- Under and behind the refrigerator, especially the motor housing
- In the void behind and beneath the dishwasher
- Inside cabinet corners, hinge recesses and the gap behind drawer runners
- Under the sink, around the trap and the shutoffs
- In the seams of small appliances: microwave, toaster, coffee maker
- Behind cover plates and in the recesses around plumbing penetrations

The inspection is a flashlight job in those spaces, and the density of what turns up there decides the programme.

## Bait first

Baiting is the backbone because it uses the insect's own behaviour. A cockroach that feeds on bait returns to its harbourage, and the material moves through the population by contact and by feeding. Repellent sprays do the opposite: they break the harbourage apart and scatter survivors into places you have not treated, which in an apartment building means the next unit.

Bait is placed in the harbourages, not smeared along baseboards. Growth regulators go in where the population is established, to stop the next generation reaching maturity.

## The sanitation half

Cockroaches persist where they can feed between visits. The items that matter most:

1. Clean under and behind the appliances, not just the visible floor.
2. Fix the dripping trap or shutoff — a cockroach can go longer without food than without water.
3. Store dry goods in sealed containers, and take rubbish out nightly.
4. Clear cardboard. Corrugated cardboard is harbourage and it arrives with deliveries.
5. Keep pet food dishes off the floor overnight.

We put these in the written report so a landlord, a tenant or a manager can see exactly what belongs to them.

## Rentals, multi-unit and food premises

In a multi-unit building, treating a single unit is a temporary result. Adjacent units should be inspected, and treated where evidence shows movement. For food premises, the treatment plan works around service hours and the product placement respects food-contact surfaces.

Free inspection and quote, follow-up rounds built into the plan, and a written sanitation scope that tells everyone involved what their part is.

## How long it takes, honestly

A light population in a single-family home is usually resolved in two rounds plus a verification visit. A heavy population in an apartment with neighbouring units involved takes longer — typically three rounds across six to ten weeks, and the timeline depends far more on access and sanitation than on the products used. Anyone quoting a one-visit resolution for an established German cockroach population is describing something the insect's reproductive cycle does not allow.

## The follow-up interval

Follow-up lands two to four weeks after the first treatment, set by how heavy the population was rather than by a fixed diary date. That interval exists because of the egg case: it is carried until shortly before it hatches, so the generation that matters emerges after the first visit.

## What a verification visit checks

Monitors placed at the first visit are read at every subsequent one. Empty monitors in the harbourage zones, no fresh evidence behind the appliances, and no new sightings reported by the household across two weeks is the standard for closing a job. We do not close on the absence of complaints alone.

## How a German cockroach population grows

A single female carries an egg case containing roughly thirty to forty eggs, and she carries it until shortly before it hatches — which is why an application that kills adults does not end an infestation. The nymphs that emerge afterwards are the population you are actually fighting, and they reach maturity in a matter of weeks in a warm kitchen.

That biology explains three things people find frustrating. It explains why the problem seems to appear overnight, when in reality it was building behind an appliance for months. It explains why one treatment looks like it worked and then does not. And it explains why density matters so much to the timeline: a kitchen with a handful of insects is a different job from a kitchen with several generations living behind the dishwasher.

## What the inspection records

- Where the live insects are, by room and by harbourage
- Where the monitors are placed, numbered so the next visit is comparable
- The moisture points: leaking trap, sweating pipe, condensation behind the fridge
- The food points: crumbs under the range, pet dishes, open storage, bin practice
- Structural items: gaps around plumbing, loose cover plates, damaged cabinet backs
- Whether the unit shares walls, plumbing or ventilation with another unit

## What the household does between visits

1. Do not spray anything. Retail products scatter the population into untreated voids, and they can render bait unattractive in the places it was placed.
2. Leave the bait alone. It does not look like much and it is doing the work.
3. Clean methodically rather than obsessively: under and behind appliances, inside cabinet corners, and the floor joint at the kick space.
4. Keep a note of where insects are seen and when. That log is genuinely useful at the next visit.
5. Report a sighting in a new room immediately.

## Larger cockroaches that wander in

Not every roach in a Maine house is a German cockroach. Larger species — the ones people find in a basement, near a floor drain, or after heavy rain — are usually occasional invaders from outside or from a crawlspace, and they are a very different and much easier job: find the entry, address the moisture, and they stop. We identify the species before recommending anything, because treating an occasional invader like an infestation wastes your money.

## Pricing and what changes it

A cockroach quote is built from density, the number of rooms or units involved, and how much of the sanitation work the household or the business can do between visits. A light population in one kitchen is a short job. A heavy population across a shared wall is a programme. We inspect before quoting, and the follow-up rounds are in the price rather than added after.

## Working around a business

Where the premises is a restaurant, a café or a food store, treatment is scheduled outside trading hours, product placement respects food-contact surfaces and preparation areas, and monitoring is placed where cleaning will not disturb it. The written record is kept in a form that can be handed to an inspector.

## The difference between control and elimination

Control means the population is suppressed and stays suppressed while the programme runs. Elimination means it is gone and monitors stay empty without ongoing treatment. Both are achievable in a single-family home. In a multi-unit building, elimination in one unit is only realistic when the neighbouring units are clear too, which is why we are careful about the word.

Ask any company which of the two they are quoting for. It changes the price and it changes what you are entitled to expect.

## What we leave behind after the final visit

Numbered monitors in the harbourage zones, the written sanitation and structural list with anything outstanding flagged, and a note of what was applied and where. If activity reappears, that record is where the next visit starts.
