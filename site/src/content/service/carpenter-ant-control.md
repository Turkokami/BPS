Carpenter ants are the pest this business gets called for most often in spring, and the one most commonly half-treated. The difference between an ant problem that ends and one that repeats every May is almost never the product. It is whether anyone worked out where the colony actually lives and what made that spot attractive.

## Nesting or passing through

| What you are seeing | What it usually means | What the job is |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Large black ants indoors in February or March | A colony inside the structure — nothing forages in from outside in a Maine winter | Locate the galleries, treat the colony, correct the moisture |
| Coarse sawdust below a window, sill or ceiling seam | Active excavation right above it | Structural inspection, treatment, then repair |
| Winged ants at an interior window in spring | A mature colony producing swarmers indoors | Same as above, and the source is close |
| A steady trail along a counter in July | Foraging from outside | Exterior treatment and entry points |
| Ants under a sink or in a bathroom | Moisture before food | Find the leak first |

## Where the water usually is

1. Window and door seals that have failed
2. A gutter line overflowing onto a sill or corner board
3. Framing behind a shower or tub surround
4. A porch post or deck ledger sitting in ground or standing water
5. Roof deck under an old ice-dam leak
6. A crawlspace with no vapour barrier

Every one of those is named and photographed in the report. Where the repair is carpentry or roofing rather than pest control, we say so instead of treating around it.

## Satellite colonies, and why the exterior walk matters

A mature carpenter ant colony rarely lives in one place. The parent nest — with the queen — is often outdoors in a stump, a dead limb, a woodpile or a section of fence, and the satellites are what end up in your wall, your window frame or your porch column. Kill only the satellite and the parent replaces it.

That is why the inspection covers the tree line, any wood in ground contact, stumps within foraging distance, and branches touching the roof, before anything is quoted.

## What a treatment visit looks like

- Confirm the species from the insect rather than the description
- Follow the trail rather than the sighting — foragers travel along framing, wiring and pipe runs
- Treat the exterior of the structure where the entry and activity are
- Gel bait and traps inside, placed in the harbourages rather than smeared along baseboards
- Inject the galleries directly where they can be reached
- Write down the conditions keeping the house attractive

## After treatment

Expect a few days of increased activity as the colony is disturbed and foragers redistribute. That is normal. Wipe away the frass after the visit so that new frass means something, and judge the result on the two to three weeks that follow rather than the first afternoon.

## The seasonal shape of it in Maine

Activity indoors in February or March means an interior colony. Late April through June is the swarm and expansion window, when winged reproductives appear and new colonies establish. Midsummer is foraging season, when a kitchen trail is usually an exterior problem. By September the colony is provisioning rather than expanding, and the treatment timing shifts with it.

That calendar is why a single spray in June often looks like a success and reads like a failure the following spring.
