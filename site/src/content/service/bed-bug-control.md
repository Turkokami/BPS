Bed bugs are the pest people are most reluctant to call about, which is exactly why they spread. They are not a hygiene problem and they are not a comment on your housekeeping. They travel on luggage, on second-hand furniture, in moving boxes, and between adjoining apartments through wall voids and shared utility runs.

## First, confirm it

A large share of bed bug calls are not bed bugs. Fleas, mites, dry skin, contact reactions and even anxiety about a news story all produce the same phone call. Confirmation means finding one of the following:

- Live insects in the seams of the mattress, the folds of the box spring fabric, or the frame joints
- Cast skins, which look like empty translucent shells, in the same places
- Eggs — pearly white, about a millimetre, glued in clusters into cracks
- Fecal spotting: dark, ink-like dots that bleed slightly into fabric, along seams and behind the headboard
- Live insects in the joints of upholstered furniture, the underside of a night table, or the edge of the carpet against the baseboard

If none of that is present, we do not treat. We say what we did not find and what else may be biting, and nobody pays for an application that was never going to solve their problem.

## Preparation, honestly scoped

Preparation lists that run to three pages produce households that cannot comply and treatments that fail. The list that matters is short:

1. Bag and launder bedding and worn clothing on the hottest cycle the fabric allows, then dry on high heat.
2. Keep laundered items bagged and out of the treated rooms until treatment is complete.
3. Clear clutter from floors and from under beds so surfaces can be reached and inspected.
4. Do not move furniture or belongings to another room — that is how a one-room infestation becomes a whole-house infestation.
5. Vacuum, then remove the vacuum contents to an outdoor bin immediately.

## Treatment rounds

The first round targets the harbourages found during inspection: bed frame joints, headboard, box spring, furniture voids, baseboard gaps, and anything else the evidence identified. Encasements go on the mattress and box spring, which converts them from a hiding place into a sealed unit.

The second round, two to three weeks later, exists because eggs hatch after the first application. Skipping it is the single most common reason a bed bug job fails and has to be started over.

A verification visit closes the job. Where activity is still present, we say so and continue rather than declaring it finished.

## What the inspection actually looks like

A bed bug inspection is slow and physical. The mattress comes off the box spring, the box spring dust cover is checked and often opened, the bed frame joints are examined, the headboard comes off the wall, and the furniture within a few feet of the bed is checked joint by joint. In a bedroom with an infestation of any age, the evidence is usually in three or four of those places rather than one.

Where the sleeping area is a sofa — and in a lot of apartments it is — the sofa is the primary harbourage and it gets the same treatment attention a bed would.

## Heat, chemical, or both

Households ask about heat treatment because it sounds decisive. It can be, and it has real limits: it kills what is in the room on the day and leaves nothing behind, so an insect that walks back in from an adjacent wall void a week later finds an untreated room. Chemical treatment leaves residual where the insects travel, which is why most residential jobs are handled chemically, in rounds, with encasements. We will tell you honestly when a job is a candidate for heat and when it is not, including when the honest answer is that a different company with that equipment is the better call.

## Why the second round is not optional

Eggs are glued into cracks and are not reliably killed by the first application. They hatch over the following one to two weeks. That is the entire reason for the second round, and it is why a job quoted as a single visit is quoted wrong. Anyone offering one-visit eradication of an established bed bug population is describing an outcome the biology does not support.

## After treatment

Keep the encasements on — they are doing work for months, not days. Reintroduce laundered items only once the rounds are complete. Report any bite or sighting during the follow-up window rather than waiting for the verification visit; a small resurgence caught early is a spot treatment, and the same resurgence found six weeks later is the job again.

## Where they come from, and where they do not

Bed bugs arrive on things, not on dirt. The common routes are travel, second-hand furniture and mattresses, moving boxes, shared laundry, and adjoining units in a multi-unit building. They do not arrive because a house is untidy, and clutter matters only because it gives them more places to hide and makes treatment harder.

The practical version for a Maine household: check hotel mattress seams and keep luggage off the bed and the floor when you travel; inspect anything second-hand before it comes through the door; and treat a neighbour's infestation in an apartment building as your problem too, because it is.

## What early looks like

Most calls come months after arrival, because early is easy to miss:

- One or two small bites that get blamed on something else
- A few dark spots on the mattress seam or the sheet near a corner
- A single cast skin in the box spring fabric
- Bites appearing in a line or a loose cluster on skin exposed while sleeping

Caught at that stage, treatment is a bedroom rather than an apartment.

## Costs, and why quotes differ so much

Bed bug quotes vary more than any other pest job, and usually for legible reasons: the number of rooms involved, the amount of upholstered furniture, whether the sleeping surface is a bed or a sofa, how much preparation the household can realistically do, and whether the building requires neighbouring units to be included. A quote given over the phone without any of that information is a number, not a quote.

We inspect first, then quote in writing, with the follow-up round included in the price rather than added later.

## The follow-up interval

The second round falls two to four weeks after the first, timed to severity. That is the round that deals with what hatches after the initial treatment, and it is included in the quoted job rather than sold separately afterwards.

## What success looks like

No live insects, no fresh fecal spotting, and no new bites across the follow-up window, confirmed at a verification visit rather than assumed. If activity is still present at the second round, we say so and keep working. A job is not finished because the calendar says the rounds are done.

## Apartments and multi-unit buildings

In an apartment building, a single treated unit is a treated island. Bed bugs move through wall voids, along pipe chases, and under doors into corridors. The units either side, above and below should be inspected, and a landlord who treats only the unit that complained usually pays for the same job three more times.

We will put that in writing for a landlord or property manager, because it is a scheduling and access problem more than a pest control problem, and the building's cooperation is what determines the outcome.
